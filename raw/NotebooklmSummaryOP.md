**What is Operational Risk?**
Operational risk is practically defined by the types of loss events that banks face during their day-to-day activities. These risk categories include:
*   **Internal and External Fraud:** Such as theft, embezzlement, hacking, forgery, and insider trading.
*   **Employment Practices and Workplace Safety:** Issues involving discrimination, workers' compensation, or organized labor activities.
*   **Clients, Products, and Business Practices:** Losses stemming from fiduciary breaches, money laundering, market manipulation, and product defects.
*   **Damage to Physical Assets:** Losses due to natural disasters, vandalism, or terrorism.
*   **Business Disruption and System Failures:** Hardware, software, and telecommunications outages.
*   **Execution, Delivery, and Process Management:** Vendor disputes, accounting errors, data entry errors, and missed deadlines.

**How to Measure It?**
Under the Basel Standardised Approach, operational risk is measured to determine a bank's Operational Risk Capital (ORC) requirements using three main components:
1.  **Business Indicator (BI):** This serves as a proxy for operational risk based on financial statements. It is calculated as the three-year average of a bank's interest, leases, dividend, services, and financial components.
2.  **Business Indicator Component (BIC):** This is determined by multiplying the BI by specific marginal coefficients (12%, 15%, or 18%). The coefficient increases based on the size bucket the bank falls into.
3.  **Internal Loss Multiplier (ILM):** This is a scaling factor that compares a bank's historical operational risk losses over a 10-year period (known as the Loss Component) against its BIC. If a bank has a history of high losses relative to its BIC, the ILM will require them to hold more capital. Conversely, if historical losses are low, the capital requirement is reduced.
